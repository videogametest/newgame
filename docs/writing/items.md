# Things

The unique items, the keepsakes, and the names of ordinary things. Use the names, properties, and words exactly as written. `GAME_DESIGN.md` section 6 (Loot) has the rules.

## Rules

- A unique item has a name, one property, and a line of history. The property changes what the hero can do; it is never a plain number.
- There are few of them, each is placed by hand, and none can be lost: they cannot be sold, and dropping one asks first.
- Every unique item and keepsake can be shown to people. Who has something to say is noted.
- Descriptions are what the inventory's detail panel shows. Two sentences at most.

## Unique items

### Wren's Bow

- **Kind:** Bow.
- **Where:** Wrapped in oilcloth in the hollow of the oak at Wren's practice ground.
- **Property:** The moment of perfect draw can be held twice as long.
- **Description:** "An ash bow, older than you are. The grip is worn by one hand, and it is nearly the size of yours."
- **History, cut small on the grip:** "W.F." and beneath it, smaller, "H."
- **Shown to:** Hesper, Stane, Old Maren, and Giles Tolley, who made it. Their lines are in their own files.

### The Patrol Lantern

- **Kind:** Tool, carried at the belt.
- **Where:** On a hook inside the patrol hut beyond the ridge.
- **Property:** A lantern with a shutter. Open, wolves keep away and enemies see the hero from farther off. Shut, it is as if it were not there. Opened and shut with one key.
- **Description:** "A warden's dark lantern. It gives light when you want it and takes it back when you don't."
- **History:** An "H" is scratched into the base, and the wick has been trimmed by someone careful.
- **Shown to Hesper:** "Mine. I left it on its hook the night I came down off the ridge. I didn't think I'd want a light I could put out."

### The Lamplighter's Striker

- **Kind:** Tool.
- **Where:** Hesper hands it over when the first lantern is lit.
- **Property:** The hero can light things by hand from close by: a brazier, a campfire in the rain, a torch, dry grass. Without it, only a fire arrow or an existing flame will do.
- **Description:** "Flint, steel, and a tin of char cloth. The steel is worn into the shape of a thumb."
- **Given with** (Hesper): "You'll be lighting more than one. You'd better have your own."

### The Smoker

- **Kind:** Tool.
- **Where:** Among the old hives in the wild orchard.
- **Property:** A puff of smoke calms bees, so honey can be taken, and quiets geese for a short while, so they can be walked past.
- **Description:** "A beekeeper's bellows and a tin pot of smouldering rag. Bees think the forest is on fire and stop caring about you. So, it turns out, do geese."
- **Shown to Hale Sedge:** "My father's. He used it on the bees. I used it on the geese, as a boy, and never told him."

### The Fisher's Line

- **Kind:** Tool, used with the bow.
- **Where:** The fisher gives it once the hero has heard what the fisher saw that night.
- **Property:** Tied to an arrow, it lets the hero pull a small thing back from where the arrow lands: a key from a hook, a purse from a table, the hero's own arrow from a far wall.
- **Description:** "Forty paces of silk line, finer than it has any right to be. It will hold a pike. It will certainly hold a key."
- **Given with** (the fisher): "Tie it on. Loose it soft. Pull steady. Don't jerk. You always want to jerk."

### The Captain's Shield

- **Kind:** Shield, for a companion.
- **Where:** In the yard of the Watch-house, where Stane threw it down.
- **Property:** Carried by Bram, it stops arrows aimed at anyone standing behind him.
- **Description:** "An officer's shield with the Crown's device beaten nearly flat. He carried it twenty years after the Crown had gone."
- **Shown to Bram:** "I'm not sure I should. All right. I'm sure. It's a very good shield."

## Keepsakes

Things with no property. They are kept, shown, and placed on display in the house.

### The Fairlow Key

- **Where:** Hesper hands it over at the door of the Fairlow house, in the finale.
- **Description:** "An iron door key on a loop of new cord. Somebody has kept the rust off it."
- **Given with** (Hesper): "I've had this twenty years. I oiled the lock every spring. Don't ask me why."

### A Guard's Helmet

- **Where:** On a fencepost on the soldiers' road.
- **Description:** "Left on a fencepost, the way you'd leave a hat you meant to come back for."

### A Taper Stub

- **Where:** Kept by the hero after lighting the first lantern.
- **Description:** "What was left of the taper you lit the Mill's lantern with. It is not good for anything. You have kept it anyway."

### Story keepsakes

## Ordinary things

Names only. Numbers are tuning.

### Arrows

Standard arrow. Blunt arrow. Fire arrow. Whistling arrow.

### Materials

- **From hunting:** venison, rabbit, hide, sinew, tallow.
- **From the mill yard:** goose quill. The geese are not harmed; they shed.
- **From foraging:** yarrow, comfrey, white comfrey (the firefly grove only), mushrooms, wild garlic, apples, pears, honey, beeswax.
- **From the lake:** perch, pike, eel.
- **From trees:** ash stave, arrow shaft, kindling.
- **From the hills:** copper ore, tin ore, iron ore.
- **Worked:** bronze, iron, steel, charcoal, cloth, leather.

Tallow and beeswax make candles. It is why the valley is called what it is.

### Gear tiers and quality

Bronze, iron, steel. Common, fine, masterwork. An item's name is its quality, if not common, then its material, then what it is: "Fine iron blade."

### Crowns

"A crown" and "crowns." The coin's face is described once, in the detail panel of the purse: "The old kingdom's coin. The crown on it is worn nearly smooth, and nobody has minted another."
