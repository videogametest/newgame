# Household stories

Five longer stories, each about people already in the valley. Use the steps, conditions, and lines exactly as written. `GAME_DESIGN.md` section 9 has the rules.

## Rules for all five

- **Never listed.** There is no log and no marker. Each story starts from something overheard or said in passing, and moves when the hero does or shows something.
- **Each has one decision,** made by doing or by Show, never from a menu. Both ways are honourable, both cost something, and neither is marked as right.
- **Nothing is locked away.** No perk, companion, household, or ending depends on a decision here. What changes is who is where, how soon something comes, and what people say.
- **If the person at the centre was killed,** the story has a shorter form, given under each. It still ends.
- **The hero never speaks.** Where someone asks a question, the answer is what the hero does next.
- Nobody uses a pronoun for Wren.

## 1. The miller's hand

Dob Finch dressed the Mill's stones as a boy and has stood beside them with a spear since. Corin Marrin owns the Mill and has never seen it work.

**Needs:** the Mill freed, the Marrins home, Dob Finch spared and stayed.

**Step 1. He won't go up.** Dob takes any work in Wick except at the Mill. Corin is heard struggling.

`story.miller.1.corin` (at the Mill, any day after the Marrins settle)

1. Corin: "It grinds. It grinds badly. Mother says the furrows are wrong and I don't know what a furrow is."

`story.miller.1.dob` (in Wick)

1. Dob: "I'll dig. I'll carry. I'll not go up there. I held a spear in her yard for nine years."

**Step 2. The mill bill.** In the Mill's loft, behind the meal bins, is a stone-dresser's pick with a boy's initials burned into the handle: D.F. It is a keepsake, *A Mill Bill*: "A pick for dressing millstones. The handle is cut down to fit a small hand." Shown to Dob:

`story.miller.2.dob`

1. Dob: "Old Marrin cut that handle down for me. I was twelve."
2. Dob: "He said a stone's like a person. You don't hit it. You find where it wants to open."
3. Dob: "All right. I'll go up. If she sends me off, she sends me off."

The next morning Dob is at the Mill. Flour is better and cheaper from then on.

`story.miller.2.ansa`

1. Ansa: "He asked at the gate. He didn't walk in. Dav would have liked that."

**Step 3. The decision: the gate.** This step comes only with the turn. The morning the Mill is retaken, Dob finds the hero.

`story.miller.3.dob`

1. Dob: "They don't know I stayed. Kerr's lot came from the Watch-house. To them I'm one of Gadd's who ran."
2. Dob: "I could walk back in. Say I hid in the woods. I'd have the mill door off its bar by the middle of the night."
3. Dob: "Or I stay here and you do it your way. I'll not think less of you. Tell me by dusk. If I'm going, hand me my old spear. It's by Hesper's door."

- **Send him.** The hero gives Dob the spear before dusk. That night the mill door is unbarred, and Brock Tull is away from it for the first watch. But Sergeant Kerr is not a fool: if the bell is rung while Dob is inside, he is taken to the Watch-house, and must be found there and freed when it falls.
- **Keep him.** The hero lets dusk pass. The Mill is retaken the hard way. Dob is safe.

`story.miller.3.sent` (Dob, after the Mill is relit, if he was not caught)

1. Dob: "I stood in that door and held it for you. First time I've held anything there I was glad to."

`story.miller.3.caught` (Dob, freed at the Watch-house)

1. Dob: "She had me dusting that table. Every morning. Round the bowls. I'd rather the spear."

`story.miller.3.kept` (Dob, after the Mill is relit)

1. Dob: "You wouldn't let me. I was angry till about midnight. Then I heard the bell go and I wasn't."

`story.miller.3.ansa.sent`

1. Ansa: "You sent my miller back into that. He went, too. I don't know which of you I'm crosser with."

`story.miller.3.ansa.kept`

1. Ansa: "He'd have gone. You didn't let him. I'll remember that."

**If Dob was killed, or did not stay.** Step 2's pick is still in the loft. Shown to Ansa:

`story.miller.dead`

1. Ansa: "That was the Finch boy's. Dav's father cut it down for him."
2. Ansa: "He was up there with a spear, wasn't he. All those years. Give it to Corin. Somebody should learn."

Corin learns slowly. The flour improves when twelve windows are lit.

## 2. The striker

Peg Rudge struck for Bertram Aldric for thirty years, and kept his fire lit for Stane's men after he left. He has not forgiven it.

**Needs:** the Forge freed, the hammer returned, Bertram home, Peg Rudge spared and stayed.

**Step 1. No steel.** Bertram makes bronze and iron. Asked for steel, by showing him steel or iron ore:

`story.striker.1.bertram`

1. Bertram: "Steel wants two. One to hold and one to strike. I had a striker."
2. Bertram: "She stayed and struck for them. Don't bring her up."

`story.striker.1.garrick`

1. Garrick: "I can't strike for steel. I've tried. He says I hit like I'm apologising."

**Step 2. Before dawn.** If the hero is at the Forge before dawn, Peg is there alone, lighting the fire. She leaves before Bertram comes.

`story.striker.2.peg`

1. Peg: "He doesn't know it's me. He thinks Garrick's learned to rise early."
2. Peg: "I made their blades. Bad ones. Every one with a flaw in the tang. Nobody ever asked me, so I never said."

In the Forge's scrap heap is a snapped blade, a keepsake, *A Flawed Blade*: "One of Stane's. It broke at the tang, where somebody meant it to."

**Step 3. The decision.** Bertram, the next time the hero comes in the day:

`story.striker.3.bertram`

1. Bertram: "Somebody's been at my fire. I'm not a fool."
2. Bertram: "I'll make your steel. Tomorrow. But I'll not work in a village with her in it. See her to the road, and come back, and we'll start."

- **See her to the road.** The hero walks with Peg to the valley's edge. She goes without argument. Steel is made from the next day. Peg is gone from Wick and her window, if she had one, is dark.
- **Show him the blade.** The hero shows Bertram the flawed blade instead. He says nothing that day. Steel is not made. On the third morning after, the hero finds the two of them at the anvil.

`story.striker.3.road` (Peg, at the valley's edge)

1. Peg: "He's right to. Tell him the fire wants banking at night, not raking. He rakes it."

`story.striker.3.road.garrick`

1. Garrick: "He got his way. He's made steel all week and not said a word. That's not him being happy."

`story.striker.3.blade` (Bertram, shown the flawed blade)

1. Bertram: "That's a striker's flaw. That's not a mistake. You'd have to mean that."
2. Bertram: "Go away. I'm thinking."

`story.striker.3.blade.after` (the third morning)

1. Bertram: "Nine years of blades. Every one. And she never told a soul. I'd have told everybody."
2. Peg: "Hold it still, old man."

**If Peg was killed.** Step 1 is the same. The flawed blade is still in the scrap. Shown to Bertram:

`story.striker.dead`

1. Bertram: "That's a striker's flaw. That's Peg."
2. Bertram: "Thirty years she lit my fire. Who did you think was keeping it for me?"

Steel comes when Garrick has learned to strike: at fourteen lit windows.

## 3. The tally

Tobin Pike collected the tenth from his own neighbours. He kept count.

**Needs:** Tobin won back, the Pikes home.

**Step 1. He counts doors.** In Wick, Tobin crosses the square by the long way round. At a campfire, at the second stage of bond:

`story.tally.1.tobin`

1. Tobin: "Every door in that village, I've knocked on with my hand out."
2. Tobin: "I kept a stick. A notch a visit. I don't know why. So somebody would have the count, I suppose. It's under the east fence at the Forge."

**Step 2. The stick.** Under the third post of the Forge's east fence: a keepsake, *A Tally Stick*: "Hazel, notched down both sides. Sixty-one. Some doors more than once."

**Step 3. The decision.** The stick can be shown to Tobin, or to his father.

- **Show Ned.** Ned makes it public. Tobin leaves the company for three days and works a day for every door: he cannot be taken along in that time. Afterwards the village's prices for the hero are a little kinder, and each household has a line.
- **Show Tobin.** He burns it at the next campfire. He stays in the company. Nothing is said in Wick, and nothing changes there.

`story.tally.3.ned`

1. Ned: "Sixty-one. He kept count. That's the first thing he's done I'm proud of."
2. Ned: "He'll work it off. A day a door. Don't look at me like that, it's not a punishment. It's so he can walk across the square."

`story.tally.3.ned.after` (Tobin, the fourth day)

1. Tobin: "Bess Hollin had me on her roof two days and told me she'd counted it as one."
2. Tobin: "I went the short way across the square this morning. Nobody stopped me."

`story.tally.3.ned.village` (one of, from any household, once)

- "He mended our gate. Didn't say why. Didn't have to."
- "The Pike boy's been by. I gave him soup. I don't know which of us was more surprised."

`story.tally.3.burn` (Tobin, at the campfire)

1. Tobin: "I thought you'd take it to my father."
2. Tobin: "It burns like any stick. I thought it'd be harder."

`story.tally.3.burn.later` (Tobin, some days after)

1. Tobin: "I still go the long way round. But I know the number. I'll find my own way to pay it."

**If Tobin was killed.** The stick is still under the fence. Ketch, if he is in the company, says where. Shown to Ned:

`story.tally.dead`

1. Ned: "That's his knife work. He notched his bed-post the same, for birthdays."
2. Ned: "Sixty-one. Leave it with me. I've the time to work it off for him."

Ned is seen at a different household's door each day for a long while.

## 4. The thatcher

Hew Pollard thatched half the roofs in Wick. Now he rides the tax cart and knocks on the doors under them.

**Needs:** the Watch-house still held, the tax cart running.

**Step 1. On the road.** Close to the cart and unnoticed, the hero hears `overheard.cart.1`. In Wick, any household:

`story.thatcher.1.village`

1. "The thin one on the cart, Pollard. He never looks up at the roofs. He put most of them on."

**Step 2. The ford.** The cart is stopped at the ford and Hew is spared: knocked out, or left standing when the others are down. He does not run.

`story.thatcher.2.hew`

1. Hew: "I'm not going to fight you. I've a thatching needle in my boot and that's the lot."
2. Hew: "I can go back up and say we were robbed. There's a back gate, and one man on it at midnight, and I could be that man."
3. Hew: "Or I walk down to Wick now and never go back. I'd like that. I'd like it more than I can say. You choose. I've chosen wrong for years."

**Step 3. The decision.** The hero walks toward Wick, or toward the Watch-house road. Hew follows a little way, and takes that as the answer.

- **Wick.** Hew comes home that day. Roofs in Wick are mended over the next days, whoever lives under them. At the Watch-house a man is missing: the next cart carries a fourth guard, and the back gate has two men at midnight.
- **The Watch-house.** Hew goes back. Nothing changes in Wick. On the night the hero comes to the Watch-house, the back gate is unbarred at midnight and Hew is the one standing by it. He comes home when the Watch-house falls.

`story.thatcher.3.wick` (Hew, on a roof)

1. Hew: "I'm doing the Marrins' first. I took her flour and she gave me bread. You don't forget that."

`story.thatcher.3.wick.hesper`

1. Hesper: "They'll have counted the cart home one short. Expect the next one heavier."

`story.thatcher.3.gate` (Hew, at the back gate, whispered)

1. Hew: "Tom's asleep in the gatehouse. I gave him my blanket. Go quiet. He's all right, Tom."

`story.thatcher.3.gate.after` (Hew, in Wick, after the Watch-house falls)

1. Hew: "I stood on that gate every midnight waiting for you. I've never been so glad to be bad at my job."

**If Hew was killed.** In his boot is the needle, a keepsake, *A Thatching Needle*: "Iron, a hand long, polished by use. Not a weapon." Shown to Hesper:

`story.thatcher.dead`

1. Hesper: "Pollard's. He did my roof the summer before they marched. It's never leaked."
2. Hesper: "Keep it. Somebody should know what he was for."

## 5. Maren's second lamp

Old Maren sat in the dark for years. She says it was to save oil. She has never said what for.

**Needs:** Maren's window lit.

**Step 1. The cupboard.** After her window is lit, on any later visit:

`story.maren.1`

1. Maren: "There's a second lamp in that cupboard. I never had the oil for two."
2. Maren: "It was Jon's. He trimmed it every night before the watch. He'd be cross at the state of the wick."

**Step 2. Oil.** The hero gives her tallow, beeswax, or a candle, by Show. That evening a second light stands in her window, facing the soldiers' road.

`story.maren.2`

1. Maren: "That's good wax. That'll burn till morning."
2. Maren: "He went up that road with the rest. He was on second watch. He hadn't had his supper."

**Step 3. The decision: the helmet.** If the hero has found the guard's helmet on the soldiers' road and shows it to her:

`story.maren.3`

1. Maren: "Turn it over. There. That dent, above the ear. He walked into my door-lintel every day of his life."
2. Maren: "He left it on a post. He meant to come back for it. That's all that is. That's all I needed to know."
3. Maren: "It's yours. You found it. I'd not ask."

She does not ask. The hero may leave it on her sill, by Show a second time, or keep it.

- **Leave it.** The helmet is gone from the hero's keepsakes for good. It stands in her window beside the second lamp, and is there in the finale.
- **Keep it.** It stays among the keepsakes and can be displayed in the Fairlow house. Maren never mentions it again.

`story.maren.3.left`

1. Maren: "Put it there. No, the other way. Facing the road."

`story.maren.3.left.hesper`

1. Hesper: "Two lamps and a helmet. She's got more in that window than I have."

`story.maren.3.kept` (Maren, on a later visit, once)

1. Maren: "You keep it safe. Somebody young should have it. I've the lamp."

This story has no shorter form. Maren cannot be lost.
